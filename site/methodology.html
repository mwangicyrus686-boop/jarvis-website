Research methodology

Sample process

This page describes a cautious research sequence. It is a website preview, not a live data feed or trading system.

  1. Collect
    Review the selected Deriv indices only after a market feed is available.
  2. Measure
    Use indicators such as EMA, RSI, momentum and volatility as separate research inputs.
  3. Vote
    Compare bot observations and require enough consistent history before a consensus appears.
  4. Gate
    Apply stale-feed, position and risk checks that can prevent paper activity.
  5. Record
    Keep a virtual report so the workflow can be inspected later. No real-money action is allowed.
How to read a backtest
What the result represents

A backtest is a replay of a strategy against historical or previously recorded data. It cannot establish that future periods will behave the same way.

What to compare
  • Return and drawdown side by side
  • Trade count and sample period
  • Out-of-sample or walk-forward periods
  • Costs and assumptions used by the test
What not to conclude

Do not treat a paper result as evidence of a trading edge. The indices JARVIS studies behave like random walks.
